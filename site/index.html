Lineage

Standing on decades of linear programming applied to nutrition — with some deliberate departures.

  • Stigler 1945, The Cost of Subsistence — original LP formulation, 9 nutrients, 77 foods, hand-solved heuristic at \$39.93/yr. No cap on per-food quantities. summary
  • Dantzig & Laderman 1947 — simplex method applied to Stigler's data; 9 clerks × 120 man-days on desk calculators got \$39.69 (Stigler was off by \$0.24). First large-scale LP solution in history.
  • Garille & Gass 2001, Stigler's Diet Problem Revisited (Operations Research) — revisit with 1990s prices + RDAs, adds palatability constraints. Our structural reference.
  • EAT–Lancet 2019 / 2025 — multi-objective extension with planetary-boundary constraints (GHG, water, land). commission
  • Frontiers in Nutrition 2018, Review of LP to Optimize Diets — canonical survey of cost/nutrition/environment/culture formulations.
  • Full 20-source corpus + per-paper summaries: sources/

Catalog

Every item the pipeline knows about and its normalized price at each location. Value compares the item's combined useful nutrients with its current price in the Walmart-national, omnivore, food-plus-pills benchmark.

How to interpret the Value score

Value is a serving-independent score from 0 to 1. The optimizer gives each nutrient a marginal dollar value based on how difficult its target is to satisfy, adds the value of every nutrient the item supplies, subtracts pressure on binding upper limits, and divides that total by the item's Walmart-national price.

  • 1.00 means the item is included in the benchmark optimum (or is equally competitive at the margin).
  • 0.80 means its useful nutrients justify about 80% of its price; it would need roughly a 20% discount to enter.
  • 0.24 means it would need roughly a 76% discount.
  • — means no usable Walmart-national price is available; it is unscored, not worth zero.

All nutrients are combined—not just the item's strongest nutrient. Nutrients already abundant in the benchmark receive little or no value, while scarce binding nutrients receive more. The score is therefore economic usefulness toward this diet's specific criteria, not a general health rating. It is recalculated whenever prices or nutrition data change.

Linear program: minimize daily cost in each market's native currency subject to RDAs/ULs (NIH ODS, adult M 31–50), supplement dosage caps, and dietary-mode exclusions. Solved with HiGHS via scipy.optimize.linprog.

Prices: Kroger Public Products API, Walmart product feeds, and the Metro Inc. storefront reference catalogs. CAD→USD comparisons use the Bank of Canada daily rate. Nutrients: USDA FoodData Central. Targets: NIH ODS DRIs.

Limits: Metro Reference and Food Basics Reference are unlocalized online-catalog prices, not chain-wide minima or Ottawa-store quotes, and do not prove availability. Prices and promotions vary. The model uses one adult profile and ignores cooking, waste, bioavailability, and palatability. Foods have no serving caps.

The food + pills variant adds only the supplements curated and currently priced by that retailer. Nutrients come from each Supplement Facts label; Canadian ALA is currently food-sourced from flax and chia.
